# Code of Conduct

This code of conduct applies to everyone who takes part in Think-Class: contributors,
maintainers, and the teachers, students, parents and administrators who use the platform. Most
of the people it serves are students, many of them minors, so the rules about personal data here
are stricter than in an ordinary open-source project, not looser.

It is adapted from the Contributor Covenant, version 2.1 rather than copied from it: it keeps
the same structure and the same core expectations, and adds the rules this project needs — a
private reporting channel and an explicit ban on publishing student data. The attribution is in
the last section of this document.

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in this project a welcoming, harassment-free experience for
everyone, regardless of background or identity — including age, body size, visible or invisible
disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual
identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that keep the project open, respectful and healthy, and to
treat the students and teachers who use it with the care we would want for our own classrooms.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful. Disagree with ideas, not with people; critique the change, not the contributor.
- Give and accept constructive feedback: specific, actionable and about the work itself.
- Assume good faith. Ask before assuming a mistake was malicious, especially in a text-only
  medium where tone is easy to misread.
- Protect privacy. Never post real student names, grades, class rosters, photos, chat logs,
  account identifiers or other personal data in issues, pull requests, reviews or discussions.
  Use synthetic examples instead.
- Use the right place for a conversation: the issue templates for bugs and feature requests, and
  a pull request for code. Keep discussions on topic.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption of discussions, reviews or maintainer work.
- Discrimination, insults or derogatory comments based on any of the characteristics listed in
  the pledge, including "jokes" that target a person or a group.
- Threats of violence, encouragement of self-harm, or any sexualized content directed at or
  involving other participants — and never any sexualized content involving minors.
- Publishing other people's private data without explicit permission ("doxxing"), including
  student names, grades, photos, home or school addresses, account identifiers or private
  conversations.
- Retaliation against anyone who reports a problem or takes part in an investigation.
- Spam, sustained off-topic posting, or deliberately wasting maintainer time — for example,
  opening duplicate issues after being asked to stop.

## Scope

This code applies:

- in every project space on GitHub: issues, pull requests, reviews, comments, discussions and
  the repository's documentation;
- in project-related chat, meetings and other channels, public or private;
- when someone represents the project in public — for example by posting from a project account,
  or acting as a maintainer at an event or in another community.

It does not govern private lives that have no connection to the project, but it does apply to
behavior that harms the project's participants even when it happens elsewhere and is reported to
the maintainers.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, report it privately to the maintainers
through the repository's GitHub security advisory form:

<https://github.com/xhnhhnh/Think-Claass/security/advisories/new>

That is the same confidential channel used for vulnerabilities, described in
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) ([简体中文](SECURITY.zh-CN.md)). Do not open a public issue,
discussion or pull request to report a conduct problem, and do not post the details elsewhere
while a report is open.

A useful report includes:

- what happened, and where it happened (issue, pull request, review, chat, or elsewhere);
- links or screenshots, if they exist;
- which people were involved;
- anything else that helps the maintainers understand the situation.

Please redact passwords, tokens and other people's personal data before sending screenshots or
logs. Do not include real student names, grades, photos or account identifiers.

Maintainers keep reports and the identity of reporters confidential, and share details only with
the people needed to resolve the report. This document does not promise a fixed response time,
but we will acknowledge the report privately and tell you what happens next.

If the advisory form is unavailable, contact the maintainer on GitHub without posting sensitive
details publicly, and say that you need a private channel.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards. They may remove, edit
or reject comments, commits, issues and other contributions that violate this code, and they may
restrict participation when needed. A maintainer who is involved in a report should hand it to
another maintainer.

### Enforcement guidelines

The response should be proportional to the behavior and its impact, and it follows the same
escalation for everyone:

1. **Private correction.** A private message explaining the problem and what is expected next.
   This is usually enough for a first, non-severe incident.
2. **Warning.** A recorded warning with the consequences of continuing, for a repeated incident
   or a more serious one.
3. **Temporary restriction.** A time-limited ban from some or all project spaces, for serious or
   repeated violations.
4. **Permanent removal.** Permanent removal from the project's spaces, for a pattern of
   violations, a serious violation, or any violation that puts students or their data at risk.

Every step is recorded privately, and each one can be appealed by replying to the same private
channel. A different maintainer re-examines the decision where possible. Retaliation against a
reporter or a witness is itself a violation of this code.
